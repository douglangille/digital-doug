I use AI as part of my writing and content creation process. This is how it works as of November 2026.

## How I work

I dictate first. While I might use AI on either end of the dictation process, it does seem to be the best way to get my own voice in play.

I use AI for all the ideation and the planning and organizing, for sure. That's a conversation and an iterative process. And I definitely use AI on the other end, to help with the copy and line editing part of the job, to make sure that craft is taken care of and that I'm putting out good work.

But as for the messy, lumpy human prose, that stays mine. I have AI on both sides of this, of course, like any author or writer uses tools, but I keep my words my words.

As always, learning how to incorporate AI in my process is an ongoing experiment. We are all learning here.

## What AI Does

- **Ideation, planning and organizing**: a conversation, and an iterative one
- **Copy and line editing**: so the craft is taken care of
- **Produces critique** on technical aspects (pacing, clarity, argument structure)
- **Generates options** for concepts, examples, or phrasings that I evaluate and filter

## What I Do

- **Dictate the prose**: the messy, lumpy human part is mine
- **Set direction**: Topic, argument, scope, audience, voice requirements
- **Make editorial decisions**: Which ideas survive, which get cut, which need expansion
- **Verify accuracy**: All claims are fact-checked, sources are verified, positions reflect my actual thinking
- **Take responsibility**: Every article published here represents my perspective and carries my reputation

## The Craft Foundation

I've spent decades developing expertise in IT leadership, systems thinking, and institutional change. That foundation -- understanding what actually works vs. what looks good on LinkedIn, recognizing bullshit in real time -- is what allows me to strategically use AI tools and maintain editorial authority. Not make slop.

AI doesn't give me expertise or judgment. It gives me speed through the various phases. The expertise and judgment are mine, built over years of professional practice and thinking.

## The Professional Context

Professional writers have always used available tools: editors, research assistants, developmental readers, structural consultants. AI is the latest iteration of this. The ethical requirement isn't avoiding tools. It's maintaining your own judgment and taking responsibility for your work.

The deceit isn't "using AI." The deceit is hiding it and pretending you generated every word from pure inspiration. That serves no one.

## What You're Reading

Articles here are my analysis, my argument, my reporting. The framework for organizing that argument? Might be AI-assisted. The evidence is mine. The conclusions are mine. The voice is definitely mine.

You're reading work by someone who thinks the tool through, maintains editorial control, and takes full responsibility for what gets published.

How I think about authorship when AI is involved is in [The Storyteller's Dilemma](/the-storytellers-dilemma/).

## The Test

Does the work hold up? Are the arguments defensible? Is the voice clear and distinctive? Are the claims accurate?

If yes, the process is working. Everything else is noise.
