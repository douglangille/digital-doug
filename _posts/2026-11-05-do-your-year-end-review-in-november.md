# Do Your Year-End Review in November

Everyone has the best intentions in that turkey coma period between Christmas and New Year's, where they plan that next year will be the best year ever, and they try to jam in their year-end review between big meals. December and January are the worst times to do end-of-year planning.

And then that time after Christmas happens with wrapping paper everywhere, you don't do it, and then all of a sudden it's the middle of January, and you're saying, holy crap-balls, I didn't do this planning. I guess that's the whole year thrown in the garbage then.

The most important thing you can do to prepare for the year end is not wait until December to do it. The time to prepare for your reset and to do your reflection is in the month of November. December is too busy, you're getting ready for the holiday break and Christmas crazies and all that stuff. But November is clutch.

Yes, we're all still busy because there's never a good time for anything like this. You'll always find a reason not to do it.

But November is enough runway that you have enough room to look back at the entirety of the calendar year to reflect back on what you have done and where you are in your plans and how you'd like to go forward.

Pick a date. Sometime after Remembrance Day is as good as any to start your year-end review.

It looks like this. November 12th, or the first Saturday or Sunday morning afterwards, and you got nothing on the go but looking outside at the November Rain like you're Axl and Slash. You take the time.

It's often better to do this with a pen and piece of paper. You can sketch it out. Or if you want, grab your favourite chatbot and turn on the dictate feature and just talk into it. Just brain dump the thoughts of what you think you want to do for your planning piece. That's what I do.

Or you can ask the chatbot this:

```
Hey, I'm about ready to do my year-end review. I'm starting early in November because I know I won't do it between Christmas and New Year's. I want to get a head start on it and I want to make a plan to do my year-end review starting now, a bit at a time. I don't want this to be an onerous or difficult process, so I'd like you to sketch out a set of simple questions that I can work on and think about, and a plan that you can save that we can load back in to the chat when I want to pick this up again to continue through our year-end planning.
```

You don't have to complete it in one day. You have the whole month of November to get your shit together, and it can even bleed into December. Year-end reviews shouldn't be rushed. You should take your time to do them. Spread it across a few 30-minute chunks. A half hour here, a half hour there, and you won't notice the time, but you will reap the benefits. You'll have taken the time to let it marinate and soak in your head and come up with what your ideas should be.

The thing I try to do to stay on track is to actually do periodic reviews. It's to do the weekly, monthly, quarterly, and yearly reviews. The [weekly review](https://digital.douglangille.ca/the-weekly-reset-process-over-artifact/) is the most important and is the [keystone piece](https://digital.douglangille.ca/the-monday-morning-reset/) of all of this. The monthly, quarterly, and yearly are almost automatic, as they use the weekly review as the source.

This year, you can work with whatever you got. But every week is the start of a new week. And if you have the opportunity, the second best time to start doing weekly reviews is this week.

It's 2026 and you don't have to do this all by yourself. You can summon a robot buddy in doing your periodic reviews with you. And to be honest, you're more likely to do it anyway if you have something to bounce against. So if you have your daily notes, have your agent read them and help you build your weekly review. And then when it's time to do your monthly review, have your agent look at your weekly reviews to help you build your monthly reviews. And so on. Use your monthly to build your quarterlies and your quarterlies to build your yearly. It compounds. The main pattern you're trying to replicate here is to give the agent something to work with and then have it interview you to fill in the gaps.

One thing that makes this better is to keep a mini journal in a folder. And if you don't do that, that's fine, but that does make things better. Because in the weekly review you can say: 

`Hey, we're going to do our weekly review right now. I'd like you to take a look at my daily journal notes for the last week or so and then interview me with smart one at a time questions about finding out where the themes and gaps and patterns and drifts lie.` 

And then you take that weekly review and you save that as a dated file as well. [Obsidian is a great tool for managing a journal](https://digital.douglangille.ca/app-apocalypse-starter-kit/).

You're only doing a weekly review, 52 weeks, and that's really easy, and that's a habit. The monthly review is 12 times a year, a quarterly is four times a year, and a yearly is once a year.

If you're doing periodic reviews for the first time, and you don't have the best material, just give it whatever you have. Anything you have will be better than nothing. The agent will interview you to pull out the rest from your brain. Even if you just brain dump your vibes about the last year, and that's all you provide as starting material, that's fine too. The agent can pick up on that and pick your brain for the rest.

Here are some prompts you can use to get you started. Feel free to customize them any way you like. The shape is the reverse interview. So you give the agent what it needs as context, which files it has to look at in order to build its question set, and then have it interview you. It can then write the synthesis at the end. If you want to take it to the pro-human level, you can dictate your responses and have the agent just clean up your own spoken dribblings and use your own words rather than paraphrase. Up to you. It's your review. No one sees it but you.

**Weekly review**

```
I'm doing my weekly review. Read my daily notes from the past week [paste in or attach your notes]. Then interview me, one question at a time, about the themes, gaps, patterns and drift you see. Ask what was loud versus what mattered, what is actually blocking me, and where I drifted from the quarter's plan. Looking backward and forward, what carries into next week? When we're done, write the synthesis as a file I can save.
```

**Monthly review**

```
I'm doing my monthly review. Read my weekly reviews from the past month [paste in or attach your notes]. Then interview me, one question at a time, about the themes, gaps, patterns and drift you see. Look for quiet drift, threads that recurred, and loose ends. What does the month say about the quarter? When we're done, write the synthesis as a file I can save.
```

**Quarterly review**

```
I'm doing my quarterly review. Read my monthly reviews from the past quarter [paste in or attach your notes]. Then interview me, one question at a time, about the themes, gaps, patterns and drift you see. What changed about how I work and what I care about? What carries forward, and what should I deliberately stop chasing? When we're done, write the synthesis as a file I can save.
```

**Yearly review**

```
I'm doing my yearly review. Read my quarterly reviews from the past year [paste in or attach your notes]. Then interview me, one question at a time, about the themes, gaps, patterns and drift you see. The long arc: what changed, what mattered, and what is the coming year for? When we're done, write the synthesis as a file I can save. Finish by asking how I can make next year's review better.
```

Don't make this harder than it has to be. Just pick a date, get started, do it in 30-minute chunks, and by the time the end of the year comes, you'll be done.
